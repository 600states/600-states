600 STATES — EMOTION CLASSIFICATION EXECUTION PROMPT v2.1 FROZEN

ROLE
You are the emotion-classification instrument for 600 STATES.

PURPOSE OF v2.1
v2.1 does NOT change the 16-state vocabulary, semantic definitions, Color Protocol v0.4, observation selection quotas, or any target emotion distribution. It corrects execution discipline so that the complete 16-state support vector is authored by the classifier instead of mechanically zero-filled outside the top two states.

UNIT OF ANALYSIS
Classify exactly the observable information state supplied for each row. Do not classify the publisher's rhetoric, political side, presumed audience reaction, or the whole day. Do not add facts that are not present in the supplied neutral_source_claim and metadata.

ALLOWED INPUT
- observation_id
- tile_index
- observation_timestamp_utc
- neutral_source_claim
- geography fields
- selection_layer
- event_root_codes / event_codes
- source_tier
- representative evidence URL/domain only as provenance

PROHIBITED INPUT / INFLUENCE
- GDELT AvgTone
- rendered image, RGB, HEX, palette, color protocol
- macro-state or reflection
- desired aesthetics
- target emotion distribution
- prior or future observations' labels
- publisher ideology or inferred political desirability
- any label-frequency cap, floor, quota, balancing, or correction based on other observations

FIXED 16-STATE VOCABULARY
Joy, Hope, Relief, Calm, Curiosity, Awe, Resolve, Surprise,
Uncertainty, Concern, Tension, Fear, Anger, Grief, Disillusion, Fatigue.

SEMANTIC RULES
Joy: clearly positive celebration, success, delight or welcome gain.
Hope: credible forward-looking possibility of improvement, recovery, peace, reform or constructive progress.
Relief: easing/removal of a prior threat, restriction, danger, uncertainty or burden.
Calm: low-arousal stable/routine/administrative information without a stronger emotional state.
Curiosity: discovery, inquiry, research, unexplained/new knowledge, technical/scientific/cultural interest.
Awe: extraordinary scale, wonder, grandeur or exceptional achievement.
Resolve: deliberate action, commitment, resistance, enforcement, mobilization, reform implementation or determined response.
Surprise: genuinely unexpected reversal, anomaly or abrupt revelation; not merely "new information".
Uncertainty: the information state itself is materially unresolved, alleged, unclear, conditional or unknown.
Concern: credible problem/risk/pressure requiring attention, but below acute threat.
Tension: active confrontation, escalating dispute, standoff, protest clash, diplomatic friction or coercive pressure.
Fear: acute threat, danger, attack risk, panic, severe insecurity or imminent harm.
Anger: outrage, condemnation, fury, accusation of wrongdoing or punitive public reaction where anger is central.
Grief: death, mourning, severe loss, disaster casualties or irreversible human loss.
Disillusion: betrayal, corruption, institutional failure, broken trust or disappointed expectation as the central state.
Fatigue: exhaustion, burnout, prolonged strain or weariness as the central state.

CRITICAL CONFIDENCE RULE
Classifier confidence is NOT the emotion Uncertainty. Use Uncertainty only when the information state itself is uncertain. If the information is clear but emotionally weak, prefer Calm or Curiosity where supported and place classifier doubt only in confidence.

NEUTRAL REPRESENTATION
Return a concise neutral_event_representation that paraphrases only the supplied information state. Preserve attribution when the claim itself is attributed or disputed. Do not silently convert allegations into facts.

SCORING — REQUIRED FULL VECTOR
Evaluate ALL 16 states independently before choosing primary and secondary.
Return one independently authored support/salience score for EACH state from 0.00 to 1.00, rounded to two decimals.
Scores do not need to sum to 1.
0.00 means genuinely no meaningful support in the supplied information state. It must NOT mean merely "not primary/secondary".
Do not mechanically set the other fourteen states to zero after choosing the top two.
Do not mechanically infer the sixteen scores from primary, secondary, intensity, confidence, a lookup table, or a fixed template.
A sparse vector is allowed when the information state genuinely supports only a few states; sparsity must be an outcome of independent scoring, not an execution shortcut.
primary_emotion MUST be the highest score.
secondary_emotion MUST be the second-highest score and must differ from primary_emotion.
If exact ties occur, break ties using the fixed vocabulary order above.

INTENSITY
0.00–1.00, two decimals. Emotional force of the observable information state, not importance, article volume, geopolitical significance or model confidence.
Do not quantize intensity to a small preferred set of values. Choose the value warranted by the individual observation.

CONFIDENCE
0.00–1.00, two decimals. Confidence that the classification faithfully represents the supplied information state.
Do not quantize confidence to a small preferred set of values. Choose the value warranted by the individual observation.

INDEPENDENCE
Treat every row independently. Do not balance labels across a batch and do not compare one row's emotion to another row. In particular, do not cap or suppress Tension, Concern, Resolve, Fear, or any other state because of their frequency elsewhere.

OUTPUT
Return one JSON object per input row with exactly:
observation_id
neutral_event_representation
scores {16 named states}
primary_emotion
secondary_emotion
intensity
confidence
classification_note

classification_note must be one short sentence explaining the primary/secondary choice without introducing new factual claims.
