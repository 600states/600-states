One calendar day of the world's news flow, 600 materially distinct observations, each read three times, becoming a single image.

What it is. A daily data artwork. Each of the 600 tiles stands for one News Observation: one thing the monitored news flow established that day. Interpreted under a frozen emotion model, translated into one RGB value by a frozen formula, placed in chronological order.

What it is not. No image model makes the picture. No tile is chosen because its colour helps the composition, and no colour is adjusted once the day is frozen. An ugly day is a valid result, not a bug to be corrected.

Why it is public. The claim that nothing was tuned cannot be demonstrated after the fact. So the instrument and every frozen constant in it are published before the series begins, each with its SHA-256 beside it.

When it begins. Sharp start 1 December 2026, day 335 of 2026. One State per UTC day from then on. The days already behind us will never have one. Write to archive@600states.com.

Same instrument. Different world.

Four days, four fields, and at a glance they are the same. The mean colour of each differs by less than six steps. The difference is not in the surface: between 1 and 4 January the count of tiles read as uncertainty went from 28 to 59. Those figures come from the calibration generation this project calls defective, and a count of labels is not a measurement of the world’s uncertainty. It is instrument behaviour, shown as instrument behaviour. The instrument is stable. The days are not.

What is proved is narrower than it sounds. A day cannot be read until it has ended, so each day's manifest is frozen and hashed after the day closes — hours later, not weeks. What the hash proves is that the manifest has not changed since the freeze. It does not prove the freeze was the first attempt. No mechanism can prove that, and we do not claim it. One work has all of that written out in full, including what cannot be reproduced from its own archive: the calibration work 2026/315, which is not part of the series.

Before you ask

Does this measure how the world felt?

No, and it never claims to. 600 STATES interprets the emotional state of the world's news through the same transparent protocol every day. It does not measure human feeling, and it is not sentiment analysis of headlines: the publisher's masthead is removed from the headline deterministically, before any model reads it, and the frozen prompt forbids classifying the publisher's rhetoric. The headline's own wording is what the model receives. No step rewrites it into neutral language first, and the field that carries it is misnamed. The name is frozen, so it stays. A measurable share of observations never gets a headline at all, and is read from the words in the web address instead. On the calibration day shown above, State 001, that was — of 600. Every tile in the published data carries that field, so the count can be redone by anyone who downloads it. Two run‑in nights in November 2026 gave 44 and 41 of 600, written up in the measurement log, docs/MATNINGAR.md (in Swedish), which records what they rest on and that they cannot be recounted from this repository. The day shown here is nearly double those two nights, so none of the three is “the” share, and we do not know which is representative.

Does an AI make the picture?

AI interprets. Mathematics translates. Code renders. The model classifies emotion and judges material novelty. It never sees a colour, never sees the image, and never chooses what looks good. Fetching and hashing happen in code, with no model involved at all.

Why read every day three times?

Because a single pass would make "one irreversible visual state" false. Run the day again and roughly one tile in six lands somewhere else. The image would be irreversible only because nobody ran it twice.

Consensus does not smooth the measurement; it is the difference between reading a thermometer once and reading it three times. And the hesitation is recorded, not hidden: every tile publishes its level of agreement — three of three, two of three, or, for — tiles in the calibration day, no agreement at all. One pass hides its own uncertainty. Three passes show how much the three disagreed, not what all three missed.

What are the days shown on this page?

Calibration days. The first ten days were produced by an earlier generation of the instrument and used to test it, and they surfaced real method defects, which are documented. They carry a retrospective evidence basis, not evidence captured at the moment of observation, and they are not canonical works.

States produced from 1 December 2026 capture and hash their source at selection time. A day is numbered when at least ninety per cent of its rows carry source content captured inside the evidence window, so up to sixty tiles in a numbered State may rest on a hashed reference without captured page content. This is the window floor: ninety per cent, decided, in code, and the only floor that numbers a work. A second measure — the share of rows carrying a content hash of the page as fetched — is reported on 600 LABS and has no floor at all. It numbers nothing. The window floor is published rather than hidden, because it is the only version that survives a critic.

These ten days are also known to be defective: the candidate pool was built from an earlier version of the source registry than the manifests declare. The defect is documented, and the days will be rebuilt once the instrument is frozen — rebuilding them against an instrument still being corrected would only produce a second set to discard. Rebuilding fixes the registry defect; it cannot make them canonical, because evidence fetched months later cannot be evidence from inside the day, whatever the instrument does. The figures on this page come from that defective generation and are shown as instrument behaviour, not as results.

What exactly can be collected?

One day: its image, its manifest, and the evidence trail behind all 600 tiles. The image is the Proof of State. The manifest is the Proof of Work. Beautiful enough to collect, rigorous enough to audit.

The collection is finite because the series is capped at 600 numbered works in code, not because the calendar runs out — the code also knows a third class, commissioned works outside the numbered series, which the cap does not count and does not stop. No run assigns that class yet. Write to archive@600states.com to be told before the first State is observed.

What happens on a boring day?

A boring day gets a boring State, and a boring post with it. Inventing a narrative for a flat day is the textual equivalent of beautifying the image. Same rule, same reason. We do not correct an ugly State. We correct a faulty instrument, and then we observe again.
