One calendar day of the world's news flow, 600 materially distinct observations, each read three times, becoming a single image.

What it is. A daily data artwork. Each of the 600 tiles stands for one News Observation: one thing the monitored news flow established that day. Interpreted under a frozen emotion model, translated into one RGB value by a frozen formula, placed in chronological order.

What it is not. No image model makes the picture. No tile is chosen because its colour helps the composition, and no colour is adjusted once the day is frozen. An ugly day is a valid result, not a bug to be corrected.

Why it is public. The claim that nothing was tuned cannot be demonstrated after the fact. So the instrument, every frozen constant in it, and the evidence behind every tile are published before the series begins.

When it begins. Sharp start 1 December 2026, day 335 of 2026. One State per UTC day from then on. The days already behind us will never have one. Write to archive@600states.com.

Same instrument. Different world.

Four days, four fields, and at a glance they are the same. The mean colour of each differs by less than six steps. The difference is not in the surface: between 1 and 4 January, uncertainty in the observed flow more than doubled, from 28 tiles to 59. The instrument is stable. The days are not.

Before you ask

Does this measure how the world felt?

No, and it never claims to. 600 STATES interprets the emotional state of the world's news through the same transparent protocol every day. It does not measure human feeling, and it is not sentiment analysis of headlines: every observation is first reduced to a neutral factual representation, so the model reads the event rather than the publisher's rhetoric.

Does an AI make the picture?

AI interprets. Mathematics translates. Code renders. The model classifies emotion and judges material novelty. It never sees a colour, never sees the image, and never chooses what looks good. Fetching and hashing happen in code, with no model involved at all.

Why read every day three times?

Because a single pass would make "one irreversible visual state" false. Run the day again and roughly one tile in six lands somewhere else. The image would be irreversible only because nobody ran it twice.

Consensus does not smooth the measurement; it is the difference between reading a thermometer once and reading it three times. And the hesitation is recorded, not hidden: every tile publishes whether its reading was 3/3 or 2/3. One pass hides something. Three passes show everything.

What are the days shown on this page?

Calibration days. The first ten days were produced by an earlier generation of the instrument and used to test it, and they surfaced real method defects, which are documented. They carry a retrospective evidence basis, not evidence captured at the moment of observation, and they are not canonical works.

States produced from 1 December 2026 capture and hash their source at selection time. That distinction is published rather than hidden, because it is the only version that survives a critic.

These ten days are also known to be defective: the candidate pool was built from an earlier version of the source registry than the manifests declare. The defect is documented, and the days will be rebuilt before the series opens. The figures on this page come from that defective generation and are shown as instrument behaviour, not as results.

What exactly can be collected?

One day: its image, its manifest, and the evidence trail behind all 600 tiles. The image is the Proof of State. The manifest is the Proof of Work. Beautiful enough to collect, rigorous enough to audit.

The collection is finite because the calendar is finite. Write to archive@600states.com to be told before the first State is observed.

What happens on a boring day?

A boring day gets a boring State, and a boring post with it. Inventing a narrative for a flat day is the textual equivalent of beautifying the image. Same rule, same reason. We do not correct an ugly State. We correct a faulty instrument, and then we observe again.
